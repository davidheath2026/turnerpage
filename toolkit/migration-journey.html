First, the basics

What is a data migration, really?

In the simplest terms: your business currently keeps its information — customers, orders, stock, finances, whatever it may be — inside one or more existing systems. A data migration is the structured process of moving all of that information into a new system, accurately, safely, and without disrupting the business along the way.

A useful way to picture it: it's like moving house. Before you move, you need to know what you actually own (discovery). You label every box so it ends up in the right room (mapping). You throw out or fix the broken stuff rather than carry it into the new place (cleansing). You do a practice run with the moving van before the real day (build & load). You walk through the new house and check everything arrived correctly before you commit to living there (testing). Then there's the actual move (cutover). And afterwards, you keep a list of "things to sort out" for the first few weeks as you settle in (hypercare).

That's really all a data migration is — just applied to your business's information instead of your furniture. The seven stages below follow exactly that order.
